## Player review changes

The first walkthrough showed masonry in the cave entrance, a short and broad bedroll, stores in open spaces, a square apron around dry pillars and an unlit brazier. The revised pack uses cave terrain throughout the threshold, a longer quilt, wall-backed supplies, continuous terrain around the pillars and a lookout flame.

The original stalactite mesh was opaque. Its dark paint blended into the roof. The first correction lightened the calcite and varied its tips. The second walkthrough showed too much contrast at close range. Inspection of the first revision also exposed repeated hanging moss over one doorway. Moss now grows within the painted limestone seams, with two additional silhouettes at floor level and above. The bracket fungi project from real ledges.

### Second walkthrough corrections

The ground-mounted flowstone now contains four rising ribs. The overhead meshes own all descending teeth, with roots at each chamber’s ceiling and diffuse paint sampled from the ceiling at 82% brightness before facet shading. The upper and lower moss patches extract olive seams from the wall master at terrain texel density. Their alpha follows the mineral grain and fades at the edges. The full-leaf door painting supplies narrow boards and small fasteners through the existing aspect-preserving door sampler.

Bowl fires place sixteen flames and eight embers around two concentric rings in world X/Z. The outer radius is 0.060 cell at full scale. The inner radius is 0.036. The existing phase and sway animate each packet inside the fixture envelope. This shared pattern applies to round bowls and sconces. The elongated fireplace retains its linear fuel bed.

### Third walkthrough design

The player requested several ceiling groups per room, smaller pools and more silk on every floor, and moving firelight on the lookout surfaces. During floor preparation, the builder distributes five to seven distinct roof groups per chamber, counting authored groups and spreading the rest across eligible cells. Across-room captures of a three-to-five-group trial showed too many groups confined to far corners. The first three placements favor the room interior. The entrance chamber gains a five-tick roof around its protected landing. Door thresholds and the landing retain their four-tick headroom. Placement uses room membership, surface elevation and existing site reservations.

Six chambers on each floor get shallow shoreline pockets of one or two cells. The candidates sit along natural rock with an open dry shoulder and clear passage mouths. They reuse the basin's one-cell animated water surface, movement restriction and map marking. The central basin keeps its two dry routes. Web clusters occupy multiple chambers from the survey rooms into the galleries, with a denser concentration at the final vault. The three prepared silk shapes share the same support checks as the teaching web.

The lookout opts into one warm point light anchored just above its fuel. The effects pipeline samples three sine waves on the 50 ms flame clock, varying strength, amber color and source height within a 1.5-cell light radius. It submits the light after active spell lights. If combat fills all eight slots, the bowl’s particles continue and its light resumes when a slot opens. Prior saves default the optional illumination flag to false. The existing spell-light shader owns attenuation and wall and door occlusion. The light's influence can remain visible when the flame leaves the edge of the view. Generation checks exercise normal and fallback room coverage, water collision and clearance. Fixed-pose render checks compare the rock beside the bowl across time.

## Art, placement and ownership

The three profiles share the cave pack. The entrance, dogleg, steps and door surrounds use the same wet limestone, slate floor and scalloped ceiling as the caves. The door, supply crates and expedition bowl have cave-specific paintings. The rolling quilt is about 0.96 cell long and 0.37 wide, packed against the west wall. Both store props use actual solid perimeter faces. The supply and cache approach markers stay walkable. The lookout owns an ambient flame at its bowl's center, 0.36 cell above floor level.

Dressing runs once after topology and fixture placement. A deterministic scan of each chamber chooses actual wall supports, excludes the protected landings and avoids overlapping the ground and overhead reservations. One or two solid groups per chamber carry painted flowstone fins or bracket fungi. Upper and lower patches use different seams from the same wall painting, sampled at the terrain's texel density. Five roof meshes fit chamber heights of 1.25 to 2.25 cells and leave at least 1.02 cells of standing room. The stalactites have opaque RGB paint, varied widths and a subdued roof-stone painting. The vault combines a triangular corner sheet, a suspended sling and a wall veil. Room silhouettes and all three silk motifs are static.

The high-gallery columns and pool rib opt into terrain caps on their solid footprints. Map admission records that flag alongside backing ownership. Scene preparation emits ordinary world-mapped horizontal surfaces at the cell's floor and ceiling elevation. The irregular mesh supplies the sides. Across the basin a fifth water surface continues beneath the rock, so the curved shell sets its visible waterline. The four blocking water strips still cover twelve cells and allow sight past either end of the solid rib. Eleven walkable cord sections mark the north bank. The discovery map derives pool and occupied markings from terrain elevation and movement occupancy.

The kit has 21 model bindings, 20 distinct mesh parts and 11 material slots. Scenery admission allows 144 placements per floor. With the largest allowed chamber dimensions the Galleries use at most 88 roof placements, 23 wall groups, twelve small-pool cells, eleven rope sections, four basin strips, the crate and the lookout bowl. The renderer resolves model IDs in bounded preparation scratch and reuses the published mesh parts. Perimeter pieces pack several fins or fungi into a single placement, and prepared geometry batches their triangles by material. The 256×256 terrain tiles repeat once per cell. Prop faces use metric UVs with four-pixel gutters and baked facet shading. The mesh JSON, editable Blender scene, GLB and painting masters are kept together. [Source artwork and regeneration](../assets/packs/damp-caves/source/README.md).

The baker resizes the accepted water painting to 288×288, joins a 32-texel overlap and writes the seamless 256×256 tile and its nine mip levels into the regular payload. Its optional texture metadata selects world X/Z mapping, repeating trilinear sampling and a one-cell eastward scroll every 32 seconds. Older packs default to their existing sampling. The pack changes the prepared appearance fingerprint. Existing campaign simulation and NPC definitions retain their identities. Prepared metadata and payload checksums identify the new artwork. The review launcher builds the ordinary world bake target on graphical launch, then reads the catalog from disk.

The audio bank owns `cave.drips`, a 12-second stereo 48 kHz PCM16 Csound loop with six drop clusters and two short echoes per cluster. The active map selects the second preloaded ambience stream at world attachment. Existing ambience gain, focus, modal pause, combat music suppression and world detach govern that stream. Interactive review uses application audio settings in its temporary run. The WAV is 2,304,044 bytes with a measured −9.00 dBFS peak, −37.24 dBFS RMS and zero sample difference at the loop boundary.
